To get the most money for your car, price it using several valuation sources, get at least two firm offers to set a floor, then decide whether the extra cash from a private sale is worth the time and risk. A private sale typically brings the highest price; a trade-in or instant online offer is fastest.

Whichever route you choose, a clean car, organized records and good photos raise what buyers will pay. This guide walks through valuation, your selling options, preparation, safe private-sale practices and the paperwork that protects you after the car is gone.

Know what your car is worth

No single number tells you what your car is worth. Pricing guides, dealer offers and local listings each tell you something different, so gather all three:

  • Valuation guides: check two or three well-known pricing guides and note the trade-in, private-party and dealer retail figures for your exact trim, mileage, options and condition. Run your VIN through our VIN decoder to confirm the trim and equipment so you don't undersell features.
  • Real offers: get one or two instant online offers and a dealer appraisal. These are firm numbers, not estimates, and they become your floor.
  • Local listings: search for the same model within a reasonable radius at similar mileage. Asking prices run higher than sale prices, but they show what you are competing against.

Be honest about condition. Most cars are "good," not "excellent," and overrating yours leads to an overpriced listing that sits. Our depreciation calculator helps you see how much value the car is likely to lose if you wait another year.

A trade-in has a hidden advantage in many US states: sales tax on your next car is charged only on the price after the trade-in credit. For example, with a $10,000 trade-in and 7% sales tax, you save about $700 in tax compared with selling privately and then buying. If a private sale would net only $800 more, the trade-in may be nearly as good. Rules vary, so check your state or province.

Prepare the car to sell

Buyers decide quickly, and a car that looks cared for gets better offers. Focus on cheap fixes with visible payoff and skip expensive repairs you won't recover.

  • Wash, clay and wax the exterior; clean wheels and tires
  • Vacuum, shampoo stains, wipe every surface and remove odors
  • Remove personal items and clear stored addresses and paired phones
  • Replace burned-out bulbs, worn wiper blades and missing floor mats
  • Top up fluids and fix warning lights if the repair is inexpensive
  • Gather service records, the owner's manual and every key and fob
  • Check for open recalls and have any fixed for free at a dealer
  • Print a vehicle history report to show buyers

A professional detail typically costs $150 to $300 and often pays for itself. A major repair, such as a $2,000 transmission job, usually doesn't; disclose the problem and price the car accordingly instead.

Take great photos and write an honest listing

Shoot in soft daylight, such as early morning or overcast afternoon, against a plain background. Take 20 to 30 photos: front and rear three-quarter views, each side, wheels and tires, the dashboard with the car running and no warning lights on, the odometer, front and rear seats, the trunk, the engine bay and any damage.

In the listing, lead with year, make, model, trim, mileage and price. Then cover ownership history, service highlights, recent work such as new tires or brakes, and known flaws. Being upfront about a dent or worn seat builds trust and filters out buyers who would walk away later.

Price slightly above your target, perhaps 5% to 10%, to leave room to negotiate, and decide your minimum before the first call.

Sell privately without getting scammed

Meet safely

Meet in daylight at a busy public place; many police stations offer a designated exchange zone. Bring a friend, photograph the buyer's driver's license before a test drive, and ride along. Don't hand over the keys and title until payment has cleared.

Verify payment

Cash is simplest for smaller amounts; count it and have your bank check it. For larger amounts, meet at the buyer's bank and watch the cashier's check being issued, or call the issuing bank yourself using a phone number you look up independently.

Watch out: If a buyer sends a check for more than your price and asks you to refund the difference, it is a scam. The check will bounce after you have sent real money. The FTC explains how to spot fake check and overpayment scams. Also refuse buyers who want you to pay a fee to a third-party shipper or escrow service they choose.

Handle the paperwork

Paperwork rules vary by state, province and country, so check your motor vehicle agency's website before the sale. In the US, expect these steps:

  1. Sign over the title. Fill in the buyer's name, sale price, date and odometer reading exactly as required, with no erasures. Federal law requires an odometer disclosure on most vehicles of model year 2011 and newer.
  2. Write a bill of sale. Include both names, the VIN, sale price, date and the words "sold as-is," with signatures from both parties. Some states require their own form.
  3. File a release of liability (also called a notice of transfer) with your state, often online. This protects you if the buyer gets tickets or crashes before re-titling.
  4. Deal with the plates. In many states you keep them; in others they stay with the car.
  5. Cancel or transfer insurance only after the sale is complete and the car is out of your hands.

Selling with a loan or lien

If you still owe money, the lender holds the title. Ask for a payoff quote valid for a set number of days. If the sale price exceeds the payoff, the simplest approach is to complete the sale at your lender's branch, or at a bank that can wire funds to the lender, so the buyer's money pays off the loan first and you receive the rest. If you owe more than the car is worth, you must cover the difference at closing. Dealers and online buyers handle payoffs routinely.

Key takeaway: Get at least two firm offers before you list privately. That floor tells you exactly how much extra a private sale must bring to be worth the time and risk.

The bottom line

Price your car with several sources, prep it well and choose the selling method that fits how much time you have. A private sale earns the most if you handle payment and paperwork carefully, while a trade-in or instant offer buys speed and simplicity.
