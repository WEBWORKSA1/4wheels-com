How to use this tire size calculator

  1. Find your current tire size on the tire sidewall or on the placard inside the driver's door jamb.
  2. Enter it as the original size, either metric (such as 265/70R17) or flotation (such as 35x12.50R17).
  3. Enter the size you're thinking of switching to.
  4. Compare diameter, sidewall, circumference and revolutions per mile for both sizes.
  5. Check the diameter difference and the speedometer error before you buy.

How it's calculated

For a metric size, the sidewall height in millimeters is the width × the aspect ratio ÷ 100. The overall diameter is two sidewalls plus the wheel: 2 × sidewall + rim diameter × 25.4. The circumference is π × diameter, and revolutions per mile = 1,609,344 ÷ circumference in millimeters, since a mile is 1,609,344 mm. Flotation sizes state the overall diameter directly in inches, so 35x12.50R17 is a 35-inch tire, 12.5 inches wide, on a 17-inch wheel.

To compare sizes, the calculator finds the percentage difference in diameter. Speedometer error follows from that: actual speed = indicated speed × new diameter ÷ old diameter.

Example: a 265/70R17 has a sidewall of 185.5 mm and a diameter of 802.8 mm (31.6 in). It turns about 638 times per mile. A 285/70R17 has a 199.5 mm sidewall and an 830.8 mm (32.7 in) diameter, turning about 617 times per mile. That's 3.5% larger, so when your speedometer shows 60 mph, you're actually doing about 62.1 mph. Jumping to a 35-inch tire (889 mm) is 10.7% larger than the original, which turns an indicated 60 mph into about 66.4 mph.

Choosing a new tire size

  • Stay within about 3% of the original diameter unless you plan to recalibrate the speedometer.
  • Check the load index and speed rating. A replacement should at least match the ratings on your door placard.
  • Confirm clearance at full steering lock and full suspension compression, not just when the vehicle is parked.
  • Expect taller tires to reduce acceleration and fuel economy slightly, and possibly change how your transmission shifts.
  • Replace tires in matched sets on all-wheel-drive vehicles, where different diameters can strain the drivetrain.
  • Keep the inflation pressure your vehicle maker recommends, not the maximum printed on the sidewall.

Good to know: These are nominal dimensions. Real tires of the same size vary slightly by brand and tread depth, and the diameter shrinks a little as the tread wears.
